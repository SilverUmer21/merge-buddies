# Merge families — expandable design catalog

2026-09-22. The user clarified that logical merges can transform an ingredient into a crafted result, specifically apples toward apple pie. This replaces the initial quantity-only interpretation. Exact stages below are proposals, not user-approved sprites.

## Selection rule

Each family needs a familiar starting object, understandable intermediate stages, and a rewarding final silhouette. A player should be able to explain the connection in one sentence. Use short action cues when useful. The merge abstracts the buddies doing the work; it need not simulate every ingredient, tool, or unit of mass.

Two identical stage items advance one step. Different families do not mix in the initial rules. Thus the art can suggest a recipe without introducing a second multi-ingredient system. Chain length is data-defined; four is the first production experiment, not a limit on all future chains.

These are an idea bank, not a commitment to ship all families in V1. Broader variety is desirable; production starts with one complete chain to establish reusable quality standards. Expand the V1 count deliberately once the first slice reveals asset effort and board readability.

## First chain: art and motion brief

- Apple: plump red body, broad green leaf, short brown stem. One bold silhouette.
- Sliced apples: cream wedges with red peel, loosely fanned on a compact wooden board. Preserve recognizable apple colors; no knife needed on the icon.
- Filling: a squat cream mixing bowl with chunky apple pieces and a little cinnamon-colored gloss. Bowl silhouette and visible mixture distinguish it from slices.
- Apple pie: thick golden crust, broad lattice strips, a warm red-gold hint of filling, one restrained steam curl on discovery. No miniature bakery scene.

Merge cues: Slice uses a quick separation/fan motion, Mix a small bowl turn, Bake a warm brief reveal and settling crust. All are representations, not instructions to operate kitchen tools. Avoid adding mandatory waiting times or extra inventory just to justify a familiar process.

Review every stage at 52–58 logical units on the real tile. If the filling bowl looks like soup or the pie looks like bread, fix its identifying details before adding more content. User approval applies to the whole chain after primary review, not just its attractive final item.
